# CHARLIE BARKLEY: design

A momentum platformer in 13 stages: six zones of two acts and a finale. Amber Downs and Chrome Works are
built; the rest is planned here.

## Story

It is moving day. The family's van is overloaded and its back latch is broken.

The Marmalade Mob, a gang of cats under Baron Marmalade, follows the van and takes whatever falls out.

In each zone a keepsake falls, a cat makes off with it, and Charlie jumps out after it. He wins it back in a
chase and catches the family at their next stop, which is the goal scene the game has now.

At the new house the Mob takes the key. Charlie chases the Baron up the town clock and brings it back. The
family goes in, and Charlie gets his bed by the window.

The family is the one already drawn: the Pomchi, the little one, the big one with her guitar, the fair one and
the bearded one.

## Intro

The backstory plays as a wordless animation before the title, 30 bars at 120, a minute. Space skips it.
Scenes cut on the tune's bar count, so picture and music cannot drift. Its last bar is the title tune's first.

| Bars | Seen | Heard |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Dawn on the Downs. The old house, the van at the gate, boxes piled high. The family at the door, the last boxes thrown aboard; Charlie circles them and jumps in. | The family tune, the big one's guitar alone. |
| 5–8 | Everyone aboard. The van pulls out, sagging. Charlie's head is out of the window, ears flying. | Bass and brushed drums join; the tune again, fuller. |
| 9–12 | A hedge. Pairs of eyes. The Mob steps out, the Baron last, and points at the van. They follow in a rattling milk float. | The Mob's motif: low clavinet and bass, swung. |
| 13–16 | A bump. The latch pops and the picnic basket bounces out. Eyes in a hedge; Biscuit leaps out and runs with it. | A stop-time hit on the bump, then the motif faster. |
| 17–20 | Charlie sees it through the back window and barks. Nobody hears. He squeezes out of the door and jumps. | The first phrase of Charlie's theme. |
| 21–24 | He lands rolling and shakes. Biscuit goes by with the basket, then the Mob on its float, all the way the van went. He looks after them and barks. | The two motifs, a bar each, call and answer. |
| 25–28 | He winds up, as the player will, and launches after the cat. | Charlie's theme in full, rising. |
| 29–30 | The title over the Downs. | The title tune begins. |

Between zones the bump plays again, four bars: the next zone's backdrop, keepsake and cat.

It is a script of cues in the game's DATA, a row each: what, from which beat to which, from where to where. Its
pictures: the van, the old house, the boxes, a hedge, the milk float, the Baron, the basket, Charlie at the
window.

## Charlie

His moves are the ones he has: run, jump curled, roll, and the wind-up launch.

He gains one, the bark. It makes a cat flinch from the first stage, and each zone gives it another use: it is
the one verb whose meaning grows.

| Zone | What a bark does |
|---|---|
| Amber Downs | a prowler flinches |
| Saltwick Sands | a crab hides in its shell and is a step; a gull drops the bones it took |
| Whitecap Pass | icicles fall; a loaded ledge slides |
| Russet Wood | fireflies wake and light the ground |
| Lantern Quarter | a pouncer leaps early; a rope-walker wobbles |
| Clock Tower | a bell rings and stuns what is near |

## How the game grows

Every zone changes one rule of movement and brings enemies that test it.

Every act holds one thing never seen before, one earlier thing in a new pairing, and one set piece of its own.

A zone's two acts differ in shape, not only in hour: act 1 teaches the rule with room to fail, act 2 turns it
round and ends in the chase.

No chase is like another. Each is fought with its zone's rule.

Challenge rises four ways. Room to recover after a hazard shrinks. Enemies come mixed, not alone. Checkpoints
go from four an act to two. Pits appear from Chrome Works on and widen.

Every act keeps a high road that needs kept speed and a low road that is safe and slow.

An act takes two to three minutes at speed; the game about an hour.

Bones are as now. Each act hides three golden bones on its hard roads, 39 in all. Finding them all gives the
longer ending.

Progress is saved at each zone.

## Zones

### 1. Amber Downs (built)

- Rule: speed on open slopes.
- Gadgets: loops, ribbons, pipes, rafts, springs, boosts.
- Enemies: prowlers, cats that walk a beat; thorns.
- Act 1, dawn: a rolling run with room after every hazard.
- Act 2, evening: the roads split high and low.
- Chase (built): Biscuit's honey cart, a straight race downhill from two pipes. Rolled into three times it
  breaks and leaves the picnic basket. It waits at the race's end for whoever could not catch it.
- Look: honeycomb soil under turf. Amber, leaf green, lilac. Peaks, downs, the lake.
- Music: the tune it has, in D at 140: horns, organ, clavinet. Evening: the organ throughout, lighter drums.

### 2. Chrome Works (built)

- Rule: gadgets throw Charlie, and the way goes up as well as along.
- Gadgets: jets, bumpers, spinners, hoops, lifts, cables, bridges.
- Enemies, to add: sweepers, floor polishers armoured in front, beaten from above or behind; sparkers on
  rails, safe while dark.
- Act 1, dusk: the gadgets one at a time.
- Act 2, night: gadgets chained, with timed presses to add.
- Chase (built): Sprocket on the overhead crane, out of reach of a jump, in a hall of jets and hoops. They
  throw Charlie up to it; it follows him and drops crates. Bumped three times it sticks and lets the bearded
  one's toolbox fall.
- Look: plated deck, hazard stripe, running lights. Steel blue, chrome, signal yellow. The skyline and the
  works' stacks.
- Music (written): machine funk in E minor at 132. A pulse bass in sixteenths that never stops, clavinet
  chugs, metal hits off the beat, a saw lead that slides. Night, to add: half-time drums, the lead an octave
  down, more room.

### 3. Saltwick Sands

- Rule: the tide. Water rises and falls on a clock, and roads open and close with it. Charlie paddles on the
  surface, slow, and stays slow out of it until a roll shakes him dry.
- Gadgets: boats, flumes that end in water, deckchair springs, buoys that bump.
- Enemies: crabs, which pinch from the side and are safe from above; gulls, which dive and carry off bones;
  jellyfish, a sting from the side and a bouncer from above at low tide.
- Act 1, noon: the pier and the tide pools, low roads at low tide only. The old pier falls away behind him.
- Act 2, sunset: high tide. The low road is under water, crossed boat to boat.
- Chase: Captain Kipper's pedal boat. The course turns round twice as the tide turns. The little one's teddy.
- Look: wavy sand strata with shell bands, a boardwalk on stilts. Teal, cream, coral. Sea horizon, sails,
  the lighthouse.
- Music: calypso in C at 120. A steel pan lead, marimba chords off the beat, a walking triangle bass, a
  shaker. Sunset: 108, the pan in thirds, the bass held.

### 4. Whitecap Pass

- Rule: grip. Ice keeps speed and will not give it back. Stopping and turning are the challenge, not going.
- Gadgets: sleds, steered by jumps; ski-lift cables; geysers; a snowball gathered by rolling through deep
  snow, which takes one hit.
- Enemies: snow-throwers, cats that lob in an arc and are open during the wind-up; icicles; goats, which
  charge along ledges and can be ridden through an ice wall.
- Act 1, clear morning: downhill on long sled runs. Too much speed, not too little.
- Act 2, blizzard dusk: uphill against a wind that gusts in rhythm. A slide set off by a bark clears enemies
  and blocks a road. It ends in the avalanche run: the ground goes behind him and nothing may stop.
- Chase: Frost's toboggan, sled against sled. Knock her into the drifts. The fair one's camera.
- Look: a snow cap over faceted blue ice. White, ice blue, alpenglow pink. Jagged peaks, ranks of pine, a lit
  cabin.
- Music: brisk in A at 150. A glockenspiel lead, sleigh bells on the eighths, pulse arpeggios, soft horns
  under. Blizzard: F sharp minor, wind between the phrases, a heavier kick.

### 5. Russet Wood

- Rule: height. The way is up, and a fall costs the climb, not a life.
- Gadgets: sapling catapults, which bend under him and throw by his speed; vine swings; hollow logs; burrows,
  dug into at one mound and left by another.
- Enemies: squirrels, which run the boughs overhead and drop acorns; bee swarms, which follow while he is
  slow and are lost only by speed; bramble, which grows back seconds after he rolls through it.
- Act 1, misty noon: up the great oak, catapult to swing to bough.
- Act 2, firefly night: dark, lit a few seconds at a time by the fireflies a bark wakes. Under the roots, a
  burrow maze with more than one way out.
- Chase: the Bramble twins throw the ball between them up the oak. Catch it in the air three times. The
  Pomchi's ball.
- Look: woven roots in dark loam under leaf litter. Russet, gold, moss, plum. Ranked trunks, shafts of light,
  the great oak.
- Music: a reel in D minor at 126. A fiddle lead, plucked bass, wood blocks and a hand drum, a flute's
  answer. Night: 112, the flute leads, the pluck sparse, crickets.

### 6. Lantern Quarter

- Rule: footing. The ground is narrow, springy or moving: lines, awnings, tram roofs.
- Gadgets: clotheslines, each landing higher than the last; awnings; chimneys, in one and out another;
  trams; lantern lines to slide.
- Enemies: pouncers, eyes in a bin and a leap as he nears; rope-walkers, shaken off by bouncing their line;
  tippers, dropping flowerpots from sills in rhythm; runners, who snatch the bones he drops.
- Act 1, blue hour: the roofs above, fast and gapped; the street below, safe, with trams. A tram ride through
  the market.
- Act 2, festival night: mortars on the roofs fire on the tune's beat, a hazard from the side and a launcher
  from above. Cats in crowds.
- Chase: a relay. The guitar passes from Biscuit to Sprocket to Kipper to Frost to the twins, each stretch in
  that cat's own style. The big one's guitar.
- Look: fish-scale roof tiles over brick. Indigo, lantern orange, brick red. Stacked houses, lit windows, the
  moon; fireworks in act 2.
- Music: a sly swing in A minor at 132. An accordion lead, plucked bass, brushed snare, a muted trumpet's
  answer, the Mob's motif under it. Festival: 144, full brass, toms for the fireworks, claps.

### 7. Clock Tower

- Rule: timing. Everything turns; the stage is one machine.
- Gadgets: cogs, ground that turns; pendulums; the clock's hands, a slow lift round the face; bells.
- Enemies: clockwork cats, armoured, open only when the key in the back faces him; every earlier kind, in
  pairs.
- One act, midnight to sunrise: up inside the tower, out onto the face, down the outside.
- Baron Marmalade, in three: up the stair of cogs after him; on the face, where he rides the minute hand and
  a rung bell brings him down; down the outside as the sun comes up. The house key.
- Look: brass cogs behind stone. Brass, verdigris, midnight blue. The clock face from inside, the town below.
- Music: D minor at 138. A two-note tick for hats, a bell tolled every fourth bar, a music box with the
  family tune over the Mob's motif in the bass, the organ in full for the climb. The last part turns to
  D major as the sun rises.

## Music

Three motifs run through the game.

| Motif | Sound | Heard |
|---|---|---|
| Charlie's theme (written) | G minor, organ stabs, a brass lead | the title; whenever he takes the lead in a chase; the credits |
| The family tune | G major, the big one's guitar | the intro, every level's end, the ending |
| The Mob's motif | low, swung, chromatic; clavinet and bass | the intro, the chases, Lantern Quarter, the tower |

A zone's tune is led by an instrument no other zone has. Act 2 is the same tune arranged again, not a second
tune.

| Part | Music |
|---|---|
| Title | Charlie's theme, as now. |
| Level's end | *Caught Up*, its own tune: the family tune as the big one plays it when Charlie arrives. Eight bars at 120, not looped. Two bars of guitar alone, four with the zone's lead and bass, a run and a held chord with his woof. The tally counts in time with it. It replaces Charlie's theme there. |
| Level's end after a chase | The same in twelve bars: first the Mob's motif tumbling downward, the cat running off. |
| Level's end, Lantern Quarter act 1 | Whistled: the guitar is gone. It comes back after the chase. |
| Chase | One tune, the Mob's motif at 160 over a driving bass, led by the zone's own lead instrument. Each bump sounds the first notes of Charlie's theme. |
| The Baron | His own chase tune at 168, organ and bells. The third part is Charlie's theme outright. |
| Between zones | Four bars: the stop-time hit, then the Mob's motif. |
| Game over | Four bars: the family tune slow, in the minor, one organ. |
| Ending | The family tune in full, 16 bars, a voice joining each row. Then Charlie's theme under the credits. The longer ending adds the Mob's motif turned major. |

Golden bone, extra life and bark are sound effects, not tunes.

## Endings

The key turns. The family goes in. Charlie's bed is put by the window and the big one plays the tune.

With all 39 golden bones: the Baron's kittens are found in the garden shed, and the family leaves them a
bowl.

## Art

Flat tones on hue-shifted ramps, placed by rule or by hand. No dither, no renders.

One ground pattern a zone, on its own tiling grid, like no other zone's.

A calm, pale backdrop in three layers behind a saturated playfield, with one landmark a zone.

A palette for each zone and each hour. Charlie, bones, cats and the HUD share one.

A still mockup of each zone is agreed before it is wired in.

## What the engine needs

- Pictures loaded a zone at a time (built): what every zone shows at the start, a zone's own with its level.
  Stages are in zone order: a zone's two acts, then the next zone's.
- Levels in the data file, not the program's DATA (built): written in `tools/charlielevels.txt`.
- Songs loaded a zone at a time, more than the two there are.
- A kit named by each song. The horn, organ and clavinet are fixed in the loader now.
- A song that ends, swing, and scenes cut on a song's bar count.
- The bark, and each zone's rule: tide, grip, sled, catapult, swing, dark, lines, turning ground.
- A chase: a cat that flees along the course and is bumped.
- A save file (built): `CHARLIE.SAV` keeps the furthest zone reached; Left and Right on the title pick the
  zone a game starts at.
- The autopilot test clears every act, as it does now.

## Build order

2. The intro.
3. Saltwick Sands.
4. Whitecap Pass.
5. Russet Wood.

7. Clock Tower, both endings, the golden bones.
